About

Computer scientist by training, working in a School of Civil Engineering on artificial intelligence for resilient and accountable urban infrastructure.

Biography

Dr Dimitrios Sargiotis is a member of the Laboratory Teaching Staff of the School of Civil Engineering at the National Technical University of Athens (NTUA), where he is also Deputy Director of the PC Laboratories of the Digital Technology Laboratory for Technical Projects. He joined NTUA in 2000 and the School in 2001, and has served as its Information Technology Manager since then. He also teaches seminars in small groups.

He holds a doctorate in computer science from Université Paris VIII, awarded Très honorable, and an MBA with Distinction from Strathclyde Business School. His doctoral thesis examined how flexible information systems are adopted in industry, and the question behind it still shapes his research: whether an intelligent system works in practice depends as much on the organisation and the data around it as on the model itself.

His current research asks how cities can delegate consequential decisions to computational systems, from routing traffic and dispatching energy to prioritising repairs and issuing flood warnings, without losing resilience, accountability or fairness. He argues that fairness, transparency and privacy should be specified as design requirements of engineering systems rather than reviewed after deployment.

Since 2026 he has released this work as reproducible research software, each repository archived with its data and results so that every reported figure can be regenerated. FMA-AI trains a federated, multi-agent speed-regulation policy on six routes of the Boston MBTA network and shows that it transfers to 24 routes it has never seen, with a modest but consistent reduction in energy use and no loss in punctuality beyond a set tolerance. AegisOne Guardian implements a leakage-resistant protocol for evaluating machine-learning classifiers, with temporal splits, calibration and confidence bounds on false-positive rates.

His monographs with Springer Nature include Data Governance: A Guide (2024) and MATLAB for Civil Engineers (2025), whose case studies are now available as validated code, together with three book series that develop conceptual frameworks for AI in urban systems. He reviews for journals including ACM Transactions on Intelligent Systems and Technology, Wellcome Open Research and Proceedings of the Institution of Civil Engineers – Civil Engineering.
