FMA-AI

Federated multi-agent AI for privacy-preserving, energy-aware control of public transport, evaluated on the MBTA GTFS feed.

Route-level agents learn a shared speed-regulation policy through synchronous federated averaging (FedAvg), so that operating data remain on the route that produces them. Each agent minimises traction, auxiliary and congestion-related energy, subject to a bounded loss in on-time performance. The framework is evaluated in a simulation built on the public GTFS feed of the Massachusetts Bay Transportation Authority and compared with a centralised optimiser and with route-isolated learners.

Across 20 random seeds, with six training routes and 24 hold-out routes, the federated policy reduced energy use and CO₂ emissions by 0.78 ± 0.07 % on the training routes and by 0.88 ± 0.06 % on routes it had never seen (mean ± 95 % confidence interval, with an on-time tolerance of two percentage points). Route-isolated learners produce no model for unseen routes, which is the practical argument for federation.

The October 2026 revision supersedes the figures posted in October 2025: the implementation was corrected and every result regenerated, and the repository documents each correction. An independent replication in GNU Octave reproduces the route selection exactly and yields overlapping confidence intervals.

AegisOne Guardian

Leakage-resistant temporal evaluation of a static malware classifier, with an EMBER2024 benchmark.

The repository contains the evaluation protocol, feature extractor and tooling behind the static file classifier of AegisOne, an endpoint-protection prototype for Windows. It is published as a research artefact; the product itself is not included. Its contribution is methodological. Random train and test splits, accuracy or AUC, and point estimates of the false-positive rate each tend to flatter a classifier, and the repository implements the stricter alternative end to end.

  • Temporal splits only, with SHA-256 checks that no binary appears in two partitions
  • Thresholds chosen on validation data and reported once on a later test set
  • Detection of unseen malware families and false positives on unseen publishers reported separately
  • False-positive rates given as one-sided 95 % Clopper–Pearson upper bounds
  • Calibration reported through the Brier score, expected calibration error and a reliability table
  • One 48-feature schema implemented in Python and C#, with parity verified in continuous integration

A public benchmark applies the protocol to EMBER2024 and asks whether a threshold set to deliver a 1 % or 0.1 % false-positive rate still delivers that rate on later weeks. Results on the AegisOne corpus itself have not yet been published.

MATLAB for Smart Infrastructure

Companion code for Chapter 15 of MATLAB for Civil Engineers (Springer, 2025): five reproducible case studies in digital twins, structural health monitoring, IoT and optimisation.

The repository turns five case studies from the closing chapter of the book into self-contained scripts that fix their random seeds and export every figure and table they report. The models and parameters follow the printed text. For public release, validation and verification were added where the book reports results on the training data alone.

  • Digital twin of a bridge: bagged regression trees predict stress from streaming sensor data, validated on a hold-out set, with threshold alerts
  • Structural health classification: bagged trees and a shallow neural network, assessed with leave-one-out cross-validation
  • IoT structural monitoring: strain, tilt and vibration streams, with persistence-based alarms that suppress isolated exceedances
  • Traffic signals in Athens: green times at five locations optimised with a genetic algorithm and checked against the closed-form optimum
  • Wastewater treatment plant: a Pareto front between cost and environmental impact under a capacity constraint, with a compromise design
